How to Compress a Video to Send on WhatsApp

Published November 5, 2026 · Ubahin Team

Phone videos get big fast. One minute of footage can be tens to hundreds of megabytes, while chat apps limit file size and automatically lower video quality. If you shrink the video first, you decide the balance between file size and picture sharpness.

Why big videos are hard to send

Video size is determined mainly by resolution (for example 1080p or 480p) and bitrate, which is the amount of data used per second. Duration also has a direct effect: a video twice as long is roughly twice as big.

Steps

  1. Cut out the parts you do not need with Trim Video. This is the most effective method because it reduces both duration and size.
  2. Open Compress Video and choose your video.
  3. Choose the output size. 720p suits videos that should still look clear, 480p is a good balance for sending through chat, and 360p or 240p work when file size matters much more than sharpness.
  4. If you do not need the sound, choose to remove the audio to shrink the file a little more.
  5. Click Compress video, keep the tab open until it finishes, then download the result.

Why processing takes as long as the video

So that your file never has to be sent to a server, the video is played in your browser and re-recorded at a lower quality. That is why the processing time is roughly the same as the video's duration. Keep the screen on and do not switch to another app while it runs.

If the result does not get smaller

A video that is already very efficient, for example one that was compressed before, sometimes cannot be made any smaller. In that case Ubahin keeps the original file and lets you know. Try lowering the resolution one step or trimming the duration.

Alternative: turn it into a GIF

For a short clip of a few seconds, Video to GIF can be more convenient, although a GIF is usually bigger per second than a video.

Estimated size after compression

The exact size depends on what is in the video: scenes with a lot of motion need more data than scenes that are relatively still. As a general idea, lowering a video from 1080p to 480p usually cuts the size significantly, while 360p and 240p cut it even further at the cost of a rougher picture. Try one level lower than you think you need, then check the result before sending.

Extra tips to keep it watchable

  • Do not compress again and again. Each re-recording lowers the quality a little. Keep the original video and compress from the original.
  • Record in good lighting. Dark, grainy video is hard to compress and looks worse afterward.
  • For a video of someone talking, 480p is clear enough. For scenery or on-screen text, consider 720p.
  • Trim the unimportant beginning and end before compressing.

Frequently asked questions

Is my video uploaded? No. Everything runs in the browser on your phone or computer.

Why did the sound disappear? Some browsers cannot record audio during processing. If that happens, Ubahin tells you in the result.

Can I use it on an iPhone? Yes, as long as the browser supports video recording. Use the latest version of Safari or Chrome.
